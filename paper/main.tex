\begin{abstract}
Prolonged computer usage among students and office workers is associated with musculoskeletal discomfort, particularly in the cervical and upper-back regions, and with the visual symptoms grouped under Computer Vision Syndrome (CVS). Existing ergonomic monitoring systems are commonly limited by single-factor analysis---focusing on posture or viewing distance alone---or depend on body-worn sensors and instrumented furniture. Equally important, systems that do combine sensing with AI-generated feedback generally stop at producing advice: they neither manage how often a user should be interrupted nor record whether the advice was acted upon. This paper presents \textbf{PostureCare}, an Edge Vision IoT--LLM framework built on a \textbf{Freenove ESP32-S3} microcontroller with an \textbf{OV3660} image sensor, an ultrasonic rangefinder, and a photoresistor. \textbf{MediaPipe} and \textbf{OpenCV} provide 3D head pose (Pitch, Yaw, Roll), Eye Aspect Ratio (EAR) and blink rate, while the physical sensors supply eye-to-screen distance and workspace illuminance. Rather than acting on instantaneous threshold crossings, detections are qualified by a persistence layer and classified by a four-tier severity model, and a \textbf{notification governor} converts qualified states into a bounded number of interventions using flag-set deduplication, exponential backoff, escalation, and user-controlled snooze and do-not-disturb. A cloud-based \textbf{Large Language Model} translates the fused state into a short, context-aware corrective recommendation delivered through a real-time web dashboard, synthesised speech, and a device-level indicator, operating within bounds set by the deterministic layers rather than deciding on its own when the user should be interrupted. This paper reports the architecture, the qualification and severity model, the governor mechanism, and the realised implementation, together with a full evaluation protocol. That protocol has not been executed: no measurement of detection accuracy, sensor calibration, interruption load, or user outcome is reported here. The contribution is accordingly a design and its implementation, not a validated clinical or behavioural result.
\end{abstract}
